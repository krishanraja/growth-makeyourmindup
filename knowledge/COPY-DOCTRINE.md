# Copy doctrine for makeyourmindup

Read this before you write or review any draft. It applies
[The Copywriting, Offers and Demand Corpus](corpus/copywriting-offers-demand-corpus.md)
(received 19 October 2026) to this engine. Section names in brackets are the
corpus's own. `AGENTS.md`, `CHANGES.md` and the list at the end win over the
corpus every time.

The corpus in one line: find a change people already want, offer a credible
and different way to get it, say it in the form they are ready for, and
measure whether they act ([Final Operating Doctrine]).

## The offer, the reader, the promise

- **The offer** is a free subscription: one AI story taken apart three times
  a week. Monday follow.the.money (who gets paid), Wednesday under.the.hood
  (how it really works), Friday mind.the.gap (where it leads, with a dated
  call). Each piece also comes as a 3-minute video and a 90-second cut.
- **The reader** is the North Star's "right leaders": people who have to
  decide what to do about AI before the picture is clear. If Krish names them
  more narrowly, his words win. Write to one of them at a time ([The Rule of One]).
- **The purpose line**, locked: "I see what is coming before it is obvious
  and make it legible to people while it still counts."
- **USP** ([The USP test]): For a leader who has to make AI calls early,
  makeyourmindup shows one AI shift before it is obvious, three times a week,
  because every piece follows who gets paid, opens up how it works and puts a
  dated call on the record that anyone can check later.
- **Big idea:** see it while it still counts. Every asset carries that one
  idea, one emotion (the calm of seeing clearly, not alarm) and one action.

## The persuasion chain, for this offer

Find the weakest link and fix only that ([The persuasion chain]).

| Link | The reader asks | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Is this for me? | Name the decision a leader faces, in the first line |
| Attention | Why notice it? | The sourced finding itself, not a tease |
| Desire | Do I want it? | Seeing the shift before peers do, while it still counts |
| Difference | Why this one? | Who gets paid, how it works, a dated call on record |
| Belief | Will it be right? | Facts from the published piece; real settled calls |
| Value | Worth my inbox? | Free, one story, plain words, three fixed days |
| Safety | What if it isn't? | Free and easy to leave; say only what is true |
| Timing | Why now? | The story is live now, or the call's date is near |
| Action | What do I do? | One next step from `journeys.json` |

## Awareness, sophistication and the lead

Schwartz's awareness levels ([Breakthrough Advertising]) and the six leads
([Great Leads], [Phase 7: Choose the lead]) by surface:

| Surface | Most readers are | Lead to use |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts, Reels, TikTok, X, LinkedIn feed | Unaware or problem aware | Story, Proclamation, Secret |
| Replies and LinkedIn comments | Problem aware, inside a thread | One fact that answers the thread (a small Secret) |
| Substack front door, About, welcome | Solution or product aware | Promise, then Offer |
| Pitches, swaps, partners | Product aware peers | The direct outreach structure (below) |

Series and lead: follow.the.money and under.the.hood suit a Secret (a true
mechanism or money flow most people miss). mind.the.gap suits a Proclamation
(the dated call). A Story opening must reach the point within two sentences.

**Sophistication is high.** Leaders have heard every louder AI claim. Do not
shout harder. Show the mechanism (who gets paid, how it works) and identify
with the reader's actual decision ([Breakthrough Advertising], sophistication).

## Value Equation for a free subscription

([$100M Offers], [Phase 5: Engineer the offer])

- **Dream outcome:** make the right AI call earlier than the room does.
- **Likelihood:** named sources, the mechanism shown, dated calls that can be
  checked. A scoreboard shows only real rows with both URLs.
- **Time delay:** the first useful finding is in the post itself. The welcome
  gives one start-here read straight away.
- **Effort:** one story per piece, plain words, a 3-minute or 90-second
  version. Exactly three a week, so the inbox cost is known.

Bonuses, guarantees and scarcity are out: the honest enhancer is a useful
first read and a clear cadence.

## Core Four, inside the permitted motions

([$100M Leads], [Phase 10: Distribute]). Zero paid boosts, so the paid lane
does not exist here.

| Lane | Here it means | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Warm, one to one | Replies in live threads; next step on the partner ladder | radar, partners |
| Cold, one to one | One pitch or swap ask to one named person | pitches, swaps |
| Warm, one to many | Krish's own posts; the front door and welcome | daily, conversion |
| Cold, one to many | Organic Shorts, Reels, TikTok, X, LinkedIn | daily |
| Others' reach | Recommendations, partner kits, guest spots, reader forwards | swaps, partners, pitches |

**Direct outreach structure** ([Part V], Direct outreach structure): a real
observation from their recent work, the shared reader's problem, why Krish
is writing, one small next step, an easy way to say no. Never fake
familiarity or a compliment.

## Proof hierarchy, using only his published facts

([Phase 6: Build the proof architecture]). Strongest first:

1. A dated call the public record has since settled, with both URLs.
2. The finding itself, quoted literally from the published piece.
3. The primary source the piece cites.
4. The method: who gets paid, how it works, the dated call.
5. What is published about Krish's own work, and nothing more.
6. Reasoning from the above.

Never: invented testimonials, subscriber counts, reader quotes or results. A
subscriber or reader count appears only if read on a dashboard today and
Krish approves showing it. Put the proof right next to the claim.

**Ethical influence** ([Influence]): give the finding first, in full
(reciprocity). Authority comes from sources and method, not titles. Social
proof only when real and comparable. No scarcity at all. Forward and referral
asks come after value (consistency). Talk like a person, never flatter.

## Headlines and hooks

The corpus's headline grid ([Part V], Headline generation grid) maps onto
the `hooks.json` families. Draft two or three candidates from the families
that fit the surface's awareness, then keep one.

| Corpus family | `hooks.json` family | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| News, Specific result | new evidence | Yes, with a literal source |
| Contrarian insight | contrarian claim | Yes, if the piece supports it |
| Mechanism, Reason why | specific utility, curiosity gap | Yes, the payoff comes early |
| Problem recognition | avoidable loss | Only a real, sourced cost the piece shows a way past |
| Question | public challenge | Yes, a real question, not bait |
| Direct benefit, How-to | specific utility | Yes |
| Story | narrative scene | Yes, only what the piece records |
| Testimonial | (none) | No, unless real and approved |
| Offer, Command | (front door only) | Substack page and welcome |
| (prediction) | prediction | The dated call, mind.the.gap |

Score each candidate 1 to 5 on relevance, useful specificity, credibility,
distinction, emotional force and match with the body. Keep the best, then
check it against the `hooks.json` "everywhere" rules.

## Tests and kill-keep-scale

The corpus's test record ([Part V], Test record) maps onto `experiments.json`
without changing its schema: `test_id` is `experiment_id`; the decision
question sits in `hypothesis` and `decision_rule`; guardrails go in
`guardrail`; start and end stay null until Krish starts it. Control and
challenger are named in the report's proposal. Decisions: keep the control
means kill the challenger; adopt the challenger means Krish may scale;
retest or inconclusive means collect more. Krish decides kill or scale.

Test the big things first ([Phase 12: Test]): which reader and surface, the
front door promise, the lead and hook, the proof, then the call to action.

## Before you draft (one minute)

- Who is the one reader, and on which surface?
- What do they already know (awareness), and which lead fits?
- What is the one finding, quoted from the published piece?
- What proof sits next to it, and where is it from?
- What is the one next step, and is its link tracked?

## Before handoff (edit passes A to E, [Phase 9: Edit in passes])

- **A Strategy:** one reader, one idea, one action.
- **B Evidence:** every fact is in the piece or the thread, quoted literally.
- **C Flow:** each line earns the next; the hook's promise is paid off.
- **D Clarity:** plain words, short sentences, a 12-year-old could follow.
- **E Action:** one next step; the link is tracked and matched.
- Voice: no em dashes, no percentages, no stock phrases, the delete-his-name
  test. Then run `python growth.py check`.

## Critique rubric, for envelopes

Score twelve dimensions 0 to 4 ([Part VI, Evaluation Rubric]): audience,
desire, awareness fit, difference, offer value (the next step's value),
proof, clarity, flow, objections, ethics, action and measurement (tracked
placement and a success check). Name the single weakest link and fix that
first ([Critique instruction]). A 0 on audience, proof or ethics stops the
draft. Below 28 of 48 means revise before it goes in the queue. For a short
reply, score only audience, awareness fit, proof, clarity, ethics and action.
The score is a private review aid, like the breakout score: it is never a
forecast, never posted, and adds no field to the envelope.

## Where Krish's rules change the corpus

Krish's rules win. Each one is listed so no session "fixes" it back:

1. **No percentages anywhere.** The corpus scores and compares freely; here
   every ratio is a count ("12 of every 100 visitors").
2. **Zero paid boosts.** The paid lane of the Core Four and the corpus's
   "before scaling" spend checks do not apply. Growth comes from posts people
   share because they are useful.
3. **No Substack Notes and no WhatsApp** (Krish, 2026-10-14), whatever a
   distribution chapter suggests.
4. **Agents draft, Krish acts.** The corpus assumes a team that sends and
   publishes. Here every send, post, setting and follow-up is a draft.
5. **Facts only from his published pieces or the thread** being answered.
   The corpus's research phase (interviews, reviews, voice-of-customer files)
   does not license new claims.
6. **No invented urgency, scarcity, testimonials or subscriber proof.** The
   corpus allows real ones; this engine uses none it cannot cite, and Krish
   approves any count before it is shown.
7. **No fear framing and no hype.** The reader is a leader reading for
   work, and nobody expenses fear. Make the stakes clear and calm.
8. **His voice rules beat corpus craft tips:** plain words, no em dashes, no
   stock phrases, no "it's not X, it's Y", no hashtag walls, and the
   delete-his-name test.
9. **A reply adds one real fact** from his piece. It never just praises or
   agrees. **No links in LinkedIn comments.**
10. **Nothing in bulk.** The corpus's sequences and lead systems become one
    draft to one named person, citing something they made.
11. **Editorial belongs to Krish and the content engine.** The corpus's
    offer, positioning and landing work here means distribution, front door
    fixes and proposals. It never rewrites articles, scripts or the promise.
12. **Money models and paid tiers are only Sunday-report proposals**, with
    evidence and Krish's decision. No upsell, downsell or continuity offer is
    drafted for any reader.
