Monday: who gets paid

Why is Disney eating the scraps of bankrupt Character.AI?

Character.AI, the app where millions of people chat with made-up characters, filed for bankruptcy on 12 October. In 2024 Google reportedly paid $2.7 billion to licence Character.AI's technology and hire the founders. Now Disney has offered jobs to 66 Character.AI staff, worth about $180 million over three years, and opened the bidding for the technology at $20 million, through the bankruptcy court. The piece follows who gets what, and who keeps the lawsuits.

Wednesday: how it really works

A Reiki master wrote a UK number two, and it's both AI and human

Movin' To The Sun reached number two in the UK, credited to HUGEL, Imael Angel and Ultra Naté. It began as a demo that Imael Angel, a Reiki master with no studio, made with an AI music app. The DJ HUGEL rebuilt the track from samples and presets, and Ultra Naté sang it over a thin layer of the original AI voice. The piece traces who did which bit, and why the Grammys split the song in two.

Friday: where it leads

Meta staff were ranked by AI use. They gamed it.

At Meta, an internal leaderboard ranked the top 250 of more than 85,000 staff by how many AI tokens they used, with titles like Token Legend. At Amazon, some staff set AI agents to work on unnecessary tasks to climb a ranking called KiroRank. Both rankings came down, and Duolingo, Meta and Amazon have since backed away from judging staff on AI use. The piece explains why counting AI use backfires, and what to count instead.

Also this week

Licensing and rights

Elvis Presley Enterprises and other rights holders sued the AI music app Suno for copyright infringement in California on 6 November, citing Tennessee's ELVIS Act, which protects a performer's voice and likeness. Source: the court filing

The same day, Eminem's publisher, Eight Mile Style, sued Suno over 269 of his songs. Source: the court filing

On 11 November the music publisher Reservoir filed an amended complaint against Suno that lists 1,368 of its songs. Source: the court filing

Film, TV and streaming

Disney signed a multi-year deal with Adobe on 9 November that includes generative AI models built for Disney's own characters, settings and visual styles. Source: Adobe

Paramount Skydance told shareholders on 9 November that, with the latest AI tools, it expects to bring Paramount+ and HBO Max together much faster. Source: Paramount Skydance, letter to shareholders

Media and entertainment

The ad agency Ogilvy launched ECHO with the talent agency CAA on 9 November. It makes ads from stars' stored digital scans, nothing goes out until the star's side signs it off, and Ogilvy says one shoot that gave 8 to 10 pieces can now give about 30 to 50. Source: Ogilvy

Music

Suno launched a test version of Bridge on 13 November, an app that writes new instrument and vocal parts inside Ableton Live 12 on a Mac, matched to the song's tempo and key. Source: Suno

Creative tools and platforms

Adobe said on 10 November that its Firefly app now offers AI models from ByteDance, Google, Kling, OpenAI and ElevenLabs alongside its own. Source: Adobe

Creators and creator tech

WEBTOON said on 9 November that after it began translating self-published English comics into other languages with AI, its non-English readers read almost 40% more episodes of those comics. Source: WEBTOON, letter to shareholders
