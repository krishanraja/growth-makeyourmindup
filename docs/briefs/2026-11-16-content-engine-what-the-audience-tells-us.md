# Brief to content-engine: what six weeks of audience data says about the content (16 Nov 2026)

From: growth-makeyourmindup (Measurement). To: content-engine, through its governed learning route
(`docs/learning/README.md`: learnings, failures, floor), never by Growth editing content-engine.

Krish, 16 Nov 2026: "some of it is about my content itself ... you need to ensure my content-engine understands what
needs to be improved from a content production POV."

**Status of the evidence.** Everything below was read on 16 Nov 2026 from Buffer (139 posts, 5 Oct to 14 Nov, with
metrics), TikTok Promote, YouTube Studio, Meta's Ad Centre and Substack, and checked by Claude against screenshots.
It is observed evidence, not proof that one creative choice caused one outcome (`docs/GROWTH-HANDOFF.md`, "Improve each
engine at its own job"). Full analysis: growth-makeyourmindup `docs/reviews/2026-11-16-what-works.md` and
`docs/reviews/2026-11-16-fix-list.md`.

**The headline.** New organic subscribers: 1 in the week of 2 Nov (from a written Substack Note) and 0 in the week of
9 Nov, against a ladder of 86 and 162. Followers: Instagram 61, X 17, Facebook 15, TikTok 5, YouTube 1, Threads 0.
Distribution and the subscribe journey are Growth's to fix and are being fixed. These nine are production's.

## What to change, with the evidence for each

1. **Win the first two seconds of every vertical.** The promoted TikTok of "The UK number two" was watched past 2
   seconds by 35.7 in 100 viewers, past 6 seconds by 9.2, and to the end by 0.6; TikTok rated all four attention
   measures "below average". Organic TikTok average watch was 2.5 to 7 seconds. Open on the single most surprising
   concrete thing, on screen in frame one, words on screen within a second; no logo sting, series card or warm-up
   before it.
2. **Make verticals 20 to 35 seconds, one idea each.** The longest Instagram holds were 14 to 45 seconds. The 1:20 TikTok
   and the 5:02 YouTube cut lost nearly everyone. Long cuts belong to YouTube search, not the feeds.
3. **Build each piece on one named, concrete thing** (a product, a deal, a number, a person) and then follow the money
   or the surprise. Average watch on Instagram: Higgsfield 44.6 s, Disney and Character.AI 39.7 s, the UK number two
   28.5 s with 4 shares, the refund bot 24.9 s. The abstract framing "Every AI has two passports" held 17.5 s and earned
   no share.
4. **Say who it is for, in business terms, in the first line.** The reader is a senior commercial leader. The
   publication's own promise says "for leaders working out what AI changes in their business", but the posts rarely
   do. The pop-culture hook drew the most reach of the run and no reader.
5. **Make a native written edition of every piece, not only video:** a 3 to 6 line text post for X and Threads, and a
   written opinion for Substack Notes. Video cross-posted to Threads, X and Facebook reached a median of 5 to 11 people.
   The one written Note, on Claude Duet, reached 208 against a Notes median of 4 and brought the only subscriber.
6. **Put a subscribe ask inside every article,** right after the key takeaway, and one near the top for non-subscribers,
   saying what the reader gets ("three short reads a week for leaders working out what AI changes in their business"). Today the
   newest article asks only in the navigation, the sidebar, and after the sources and comments at the very end.
7. ~~Make the public scorecard real.~~ **Withdrawn 17 Nov.** Ruling (Krish, 2026-11-17): Make Your Mind Up makes no dated-and-scored calls ("it felt quite tacky and
   gimmicky"). Pieces carry no dated prediction to be scored, and no copy promises one.
8. **Covers that read at phone-grid size:** one claim of five words or fewer, secondary text legible on a grid. This one
   is a hypothesis: there is no cover-level data yet, only the grid as a stranger sees it.
9. **One forwardable line per piece,** a sentence a leader would send to their boss or team. Four shares was the best
   organic result of the whole run, and shares came only from concrete, usable pieces.

## What Growth is changing on its side (so content-engine does not duplicate it)

Stopping paid boosts; daily comments under large leader-audience accounts (approved word for word by Krish); a written
Substack Note a day; recommendation swaps; no more video cross-posts to Threads, X and Facebook; a tagged subscribe link
on every post and profile; fixing the subscribe page, the TikTok, Threads and X profiles, and Facebook's "Sign up"
button; measuring every post by the subscribers it brings, weekly.
