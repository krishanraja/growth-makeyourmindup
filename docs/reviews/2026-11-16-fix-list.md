# Why Make Your Mind Up is not growing, and every fix (16 Nov 2026)

Krish, 16 Nov 2026: "So what's going wrong? How can I have no x views and no new subscribers to anything?", then "I
think we need a more exhaustive list of fixes than that, some of it is about my content itself, some about distribution
and some about user journey. You control the latter two but you need to ensure my content-engine understands what
needs to be improved from a content production POV."

Evidence, all read on 16 Nov 2026 and checked against screenshots: every post Buffer holds from 5 Oct to 14 Nov (139),
the boosts in each ads account, Substack's growth sources, and a stranger's walk from each profile to the subscribe
box, done by Codex in the worker's signed-in Chromium (`docs/reviews/2026-11-16-what-works.md`; journey screenshots in
the ignored `.scratch/journey/`). A number not shown is unknown, never zero.

## The diagnosis in five lines

1. **There is almost no audience on the social accounts.** Followers on 16 Nov: Instagram 61, X 17, Facebook 15, TikTok 5,
   YouTube 1, Threads 0. Substack has 497 subscribers, so the readers came from somewhere else, not from these accounts.
2. **More posts did not mean more readers.** 20 posts a week became 50; organic exposure rose from 1,876 to 3,696 and
   interactions per post fell from about 4 to about 1. Typical reach per post: Facebook 2, Notes 4, Threads 5, X 7,
   YouTube 13, Instagram 58, TikTok 280.
3. **Paid reach bought the wrong people and no readers.** About $120 on Meta and TikTok and £13.90 on YouTube since
   October bought 27,847 views, 788 clicks, 169 profile visits, one TikTok follower and no subscriber. TikTok
   rated the promoted video below average on every attention measure (35.7 in 100 watched 2 seconds, 0.6 in 100 to the
   end).
4. **Almost nothing asks a stranger to subscribe, and the page they land on gives no reason to.** None of 139 posts
   carried a subscribe ask. The subscribe page shows a cover, "By Krish · Launched 8 months ago" and an email box: no
   promise, no proof, no "who this is for".
5. **The one thing that worked was native to Substack.** The only new subscriber in six weeks came from a written Note
   (2 to 8 Nov). The week of 9 to 15 Nov had none.

## Part 1. Content: what content-engine must change

Content-engine owns production (`docs/ENGINE-BOUNDARY.md`). These go to it as a brief
(`docs/briefs/2026-11-16-content-engine-what-the-audience-tells-us.md`), never made here.

| # | Fix | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Win the first two seconds.** Open every vertical on the single most surprising concrete thing, on screen in frame one, with words on screen inside a second; no logo sting, series card or warm-up. | TikTok promo: 35.7 in 100 watched 2 s, 9.2 watched 6 s, 0.6 watched to the end, all "below average". Organic TikTok average watch 2.5 to 7 s. |
| C2 | **Shorter verticals, one idea each: 20 to 35 seconds.** Keep long cuts for YouTube search. | The best Instagram holds were 14 to 45 s; the 1:20 TikTok and the 5:02 YouTube video lost almost everyone. |
| C3 | **One named, concrete thing per piece** (a product, a deal, a number, a person), then follow the money or the surprise. Drop abstract framings. | Higgsfield 44.6 s, Disney/Character.AI 39.7 s, UK number two 28.5 s, refund bot 24.9 s of watch; "Every AI has two passports" 17.5 s and no shares. |
| C4 | **Say who it is for, in the first line, in business terms.** The reader is a senior commercial leader: lead with what it changes for their team, their budget or their decisions. Stories whose hook is pop culture pull the wrong crowd. | The biggest reach (the AI pop song) went to broad audiences and brought no reader; the Substack promise already says "for leaders working out what AI changes in their business", but the posts rarely do. |
| C5 | **A native written edition of every piece, not only video:** a 3 to 6 line text post for X, Threads and LinkedIn-style feeds, and a written opinion for Substack Notes. | Videos cross-posted to Threads, X and Facebook reached a median of 5 to 11 people. The written Note on Claude Duet reached 208 (median 4) and brought the only subscriber. |
| C6 | **A subscribe ask inside every article,** right after the key takeaway, plus one near the top for non-subscribers, saying what they get ("three short reads a week; every call dated and scored"). | The newest article asks only in the top navigation, the sidebar and after the sources and comments at the very end. |
| C7 | **Make the public scorecard real.** "Every call dated and scored in public, misses included" is the most distinctive promise, but the site shows an "Example board". A live board is the reason a leader would come back. | makeyourmindup.ai scoreboard labelled "Example board". |
| C8 | **Covers that read at grid size.** One claim of five words or fewer; secondary lines legible on a phone grid. Hypothesis only: no cover-level data yet. | TikTok grid covers carry two to three lines of small type ("Swipe for the scorecard", dense chart images). |
| C9 | **A share line people can forward to a colleague,** in the piece itself (one sentence a leader would send their boss). | 4 shares was the best organic result of the run; shares came only from concrete, usable pieces. |
